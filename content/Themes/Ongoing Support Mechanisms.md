## Definition
The set of structures — feedback mechanisms, mentorship, continuous training, additional resources, and emotional support — that sustain employee adaptation to change well beyond the formal implementation period, often for months afterward.

## Why it matters
The series argues that change management does not end at go-live; the risk of losing critical talent or reverting to old habits remains highest in the weeks and months after implementation, when formal project support typically disappears. Sustained support is what converts a successful launch into a lasting change.

## How it works
- **Feedback mechanisms** — regular surveys, town halls, and anonymous channels to surface emerging concerns after go-live
- **Mentorship programmes** — pairing employees who have successfully adapted with those still transitioning
- **Continuous training** — ongoing (not one-time) skill development to prevent stagnation as tools and processes evolve
- **Additional resources** — documentation, knowledge bases, and dedicated support teams available on demand
- **Emotional support** — explicit acknowledgment that transitions provoke real anxiety, addressed through EAPs, counselling access, or open discussion forums

## Examples from the books
- From [[Change Management - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 4): the author states that no successful organisational transition they have observed relied solely on support during implementation — ongoing support "even months after the finalisation of the planned transition" was always necessary.
- From [[AI and Digital Transformation - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 4): a tourism SMB uses a "train the trainers" model with bi-weekly lunch-and-learn sessions, sustaining skill development well past the initial software rollout.
