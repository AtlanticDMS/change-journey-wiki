## Definition
The recurring argument that technology adoption decisions are business decisions, owned by leadership and grounded in strategic objectives — not IT decisions to be delegated to technical staff or driven by novelty for its own sake.

## What is this theme?
The idea that technology adoption decisions are not IT decisions — they are strategic business decisions. The question is never "what technology should we buy?" but "what business outcome are we trying to achieve, and what technology best enables it?"

## Why it matters
SMBs that treat technology as a cost centre make defensive, reactive decisions. SMBs that treat technology as a strategic asset make offensive, opportunity-driven decisions. The latter consistently outperform.

SMBs without dedicated IT departments are especially vulnerable to treating technology purchases as isolated technical fixes rather than strategic investments. The series argues this is precisely how digital transformation budgets get wasted — on tools that are impressive but disconnected from actual business goals.

## How it works
- **Hoshin-style alignment** — connecting company-wide strategic objectives to specific digital initiatives via a clear planning blueprint, rather than adopting technology because it is trending
- **"Going to the Gemba"** — management staying physically and operationally connected to how technology is actually used at the front line, not just approving it from a boardroom
- **SMART objective-setting for technology** — every digital initiative should have a specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound justification tied to business outcomes
- **Impact vs feasibility prioritisation** — technology initiatives are ranked by their contribution to revenue, efficiency, customer satisfaction, and competitiveness, not by technical interest
- **Leadership ownership** — final technology decisions rest with business leadership, informed by (but not deferred to) technical specialists

## Examples from the books
- From [[AI and Digital Transformation - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 3): a healthcare company's strategic objective to become a telehealth market leader is used to derive a specific mobile application objective — illustrating technology decisions flowing from strategy, not the reverse.
- From [[Change Management - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 1): the book explicitly scopes out "digital transformations" as a topic requiring separate treatment because they are too broad and complex to be treated as a subset of general change management — implicitly reinforcing that technology strategy deserves its own dedicated leadership attention.
