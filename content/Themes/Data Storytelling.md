## Definition
The discipline of translating raw data and analytical findings into clear, visually supported, emotionally resonant narratives that drive stakeholder understanding and action — distinct from data visualisation alone, which only makes data legible, not persuasive.

## Why it matters
The series argues that data without narrative fails to change behaviour, no matter how accurate or well-visualised — decision-makers "don't sway to spreadsheets alone." Effective storytelling is what converts analytical rigour into organisational action, closing the gap between insight and decision.

## How it works
- **Five-part structure** — identify audience, set the scene, present the data, draw conclusions, engage and iterate
- **Visualisation principles** — clarity, relevance, consistency, and accessibility govern chart and dashboard design
- **Narrative techniques borrowed from fiction** — the Hero's Journey structure, emotional anchoring, before/after contrast, and the "power of three" are all recommended for structuring data narratives
- **Game theory as narrative amplifier** — framing strategic recommendations in terms of competitive/cooperative dynamics to deepen executive engagement
- **Common pitfalls to avoid** — overcomplicating visuals, cherry-picking supportive data, poor design choices, and omitting context

## Examples from the books
- From [[Data Insights and Analytics - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 9): a contrast is drawn between presenting "a spreadsheet containing thousands of rows of raw data" versus the same data as an interactive dashboard revealing a clear holiday sales spike or Q2 retention drop.
- The chapter explicitly recommends applying the "Hero's Journey" narrative arc to frame a business or strategy as the protagonist facing a challenge (the data) and a resolution (the recommendation).
