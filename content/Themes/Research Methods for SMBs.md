## Definition
A curated, resource-tiered framework of primary and secondary research techniques — ranging from low-cost tools like NPS surveys to advanced methods like conjoint analysis and neuromarketing — organised by strategic business goal and mapped against relative cost, expertise required, and expected ROI timeline.

## Why it matters
Unlike enterprise research literature, which assumes dedicated research budgets and specialist staff, this framework is explicitly designed for SMBs needing to be "strategic and selective" — helping leaders choose the few methods that will deliver the most value for their specific context and constraints, rather than attempting broad, resource-intensive research programmes.

## How it works
- **Goal-organised structure** — methods are grouped under seven strategic objectives: customer retention, marketing optimisation, product development, brand awareness, operational efficiency, market expansion, and data-driven decision-making
- **Feasibility scoring** — each method is rated by relative cost, technical expertise required, time investment, and expected ROI timeline
- **Tiered complexity** — starts with essential, low-cost, quick-ROI techniques (NPS, A/B testing, customer segmentation) before introducing sophisticated methods (conjoint analysis, predictive analytics, neuromarketing)
- **Bias awareness built in** — the framework is paired with a companion inventory of common data collection mistakes and cognitive biases, ensuring methodological rigour alongside accessibility

## Examples from the books
- From [[Data Insights and Analytics - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 7): Net Promoter Score and Voice of Customer programmes are recommended as low-cost, quick-ROI starting points for customer retention research, while conjoint analysis and structural equation modelling are flagged as high-cost, long-ROI-timeline options for more mature organisations.
- The chapter includes qualitative techniques (focus groups, ethnographic research), physiological tools (eye tracking, facial coding, EEG), and psychological deep-dive techniques (Implicit Association Tests, ZMET) as advanced options for businesses with greater resources.
