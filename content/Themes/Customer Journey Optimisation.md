## Definition
The practice of mapping and continuously improving every digital and physical touchpoint a customer encounters — from initial awareness through purchase, retention, and advocacy — to reduce friction and increase satisfaction at each step.

## Why it matters
The series argues that even businesses without an obvious e-commerce presence (an airport bistro, a hair salon, a pet groomer) accumulate dozens of digital touchpoints — websites, social media, reviews, apps — that shape purchase decisions long before or after any human interaction. Ignoring this journey means losing customers to friction the business never sees.

## How it works
- **Touchpoint mapping** — cataloguing every digital and physical point of customer contact, including passive ones like social media mentions or overheard recommendations
- **Digital experience optimisation** — ensuring user-friendliness, device-agnostic compatibility, and fast loading speeds at each touchpoint
- **Multi-channel interactive communication** — chatbots, SMS/IM, and platform-specific social engagement layered together rather than relying on a single channel
- **Exit-point awareness** — recognising that every touchpoint is also a potential point where competitors or detractors can intervene

## Examples from the books
- From [[AI and Digital Transformation - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 6): an airport bistro with a transient customer base is shown to have a surprisingly rich set of digital touchpoints — website, social media, online reviews, mobile apps, and email newsletters — each shaping traveller decisions.
- From [[Data Insights and Analytics - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 5): customer journey mapping is described as revealing that segments like "frequent buyers" and "lapsed customers" require entirely different touchpoint strategies.
