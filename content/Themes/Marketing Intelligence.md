## Definition
The systematic collection, analysis, and application of customer, market, performance, and competitive data to inform marketing strategy — traced in the series from its manual, focus-group-based origins through to today's AI-powered, real-time analytics capability.

## Why it matters
Marketing intelligence is presented as the connective discipline linking data collection to business outcomes: it is the applied practice that data-driven decision-making becomes once directed specifically at customer and market understanding.

## How it works
- **Historical evolution** — from 1970s-era focus groups and manual surveys, through the digital-tools era (SurveyMonkey, Google Analytics), to today's AI/ML-powered predictive capability
- **Structured, integrated data collection** — combining customer, performance, market, and social media data types for a holistic view, rather than relying on any single source
- **Democratised tooling** — free and low-cost platforms (Google Looker Studio, Mailchimp AI) now provide entry-level capability once reserved for enterprises
- **AI-driven acceleration** — machine learning, NLP, and computer vision increasingly automate insight generation from previously unstructured data (reviews, social posts, images)

## Examples from the books
- From [[Data Insights and Analytics - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 1): a narrative history traces marketing intelligence from "dull tables in dull rooms" behind one-way mirrors to today's AI-powered, real-time predictive analytics.
- The book profiles specific SMB comparisons between entry-level tools (Google Looker Studio, Mailchimp) and advanced platforms (Salesforce Einstein, HubSpot AI) to illustrate the maturity curve businesses can follow.
