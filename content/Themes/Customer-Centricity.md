## Definition
The organisational commitment to placing customer needs, preferences, and pain points at the centre of every strategic and operational decision — treated in the series as a mindset shift, not a department or feature set.

## What is this theme?
The organisational orientation that places the customer's needs, experience, and journey at the centre of every business decision — including, and especially, technology decisions.

## Why it matters
The series repeatedly warns against digital transformation or data analytics pursued for their own sake, disconnected from actual customer value. Customer-centricity is positioned as the test that separates genuine transformation from technology adoption theatre.

Digital transformation that is internally focused (efficiency, cost reduction) without considering the customer experience often delivers operational gains while damaging the customer relationship. The most durable transformations improve both.
## How it works
- **Living-breathing-customer framing** — Vol II explicitly reminds leaders that at the receiving end of every digital initiative is a real person, not an abstraction
- **Personalisation at scale** — using data and automation to deliver individualised experiences without requiring proportional headcount growth
- **Voice of the customer integration** — incorporating direct customer feedback at every stage of strategy development, not just post-launch
- **Balancing personalisation with authenticity** — the series cautions against over-personalisation feeling intrusive or "creepy," and against pursuing only "ideal" customer profiles at the expense of organic, values-driven growth

## How different books approach it

### [[AI and Digital Transformation - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]]
Chapters 6 and 7 are dedicated to customer journey optimisation and personalised customer strategies. The Literatour parable (Emma's bookstore) demonstrates how CRM, segmentation, and automation combine to deliver personalised experiences at scale. The epilogue's second key takeaway explicitly frames customer experience as a strategic outcome of transformation.

### [[Data Insights and Analytics - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]]
Vol 3 provides the analytical toolkit for customer-centricity that Vol 2 described conceptually. Customer analytics (Ch. 5), predictive modelling for CLV and churn (Ch. 8), and the Brew Haven case study all demonstrate customer-centricity operationalised through data. Seth Godin's "find products for your customers" — cited in Ch. 7 — is the philosophical statement of this theme.

## Examples from the books
- From [[AI and Digital Transformation - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 7): Emma's fictional bookstore "Literatour" builds customer personas (e.g. "Mystery Mike," "Fantasy Fiona") and uses CRM-driven personalisation to turn one-time buyers into loyal advocates.
- From [[Data Insights and Analytics - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 4): a restaurant analogy contrasts a chef focused on craftsmanship (attracting customers organically) with one obsessed with targeting only "ideal" diners (limiting growth) — used to argue that authentic value creation outperforms narrow data-driven targeting.
