## Definition
The systematic identification, assessment, and mitigation of risks associated with digital transformation initiatives — spanning technology, people, financial, operational, regulatory, and reputational risk categories.

## Why it matters
Digital transformation is inherently high-risk. Most initiatives run over budget, over schedule, or underdeliver. A proactive risk management approach converts unpredictable failures into manageable, anticipated challenges.

## Key risk categories (from Ch. 11)
- **Technology risk** — system failures, integration complexity, vendor dependency, obsolescence
- **People risk** — skills gaps, resistance to adoption, key person dependency, talent loss during transition
- **Financial risk** — cost overruns, delayed ROI, underestimated implementation costs
- **Operational risk** — workflow disruption, downtime during migration, productivity dip during learning curves
- **Cybersecurity risk** — expanded attack surface from new tools and cloud environments (see [[Cybersecurity]])
- **Regulatory and compliance risk** — data privacy violations, industry-specific regulation breaches
- **Vendor risk** — over-reliance on a single provider, vendor lock-in, provider failure

## Mitigation approaches
- Phased implementation — pilot before full rollout; start with non-critical workloads
- Contingency budgeting — plan for 20–30% cost overruns on complex transformation projects
- Parallel running — maintain legacy systems during transition periods
- Regular risk reviews — treat risk register as a living document, reviewed at every milestone
- Clear rollback plans — define conditions and procedures for reverting changes if critical failures occur
