## Definition
A sequential eight-step framework developed by Dr. [[John Kotter]] for guiding organisations through planned change. Widely used for less complex transformations due to its clarity and comprehensiveness.

## Why it matters
Provides a clear road map that any size organisation can follow. Each step builds on the previous, reducing the risk of skipping critical phases like urgency-building or reinforcement.

## How it works
Steps 1–3 create the climate for change. Steps 4–6 engage and enable the organisation. Steps 7–8 focus on implementation and sustainment.

1. **Create Urgency** — make the case for change with compelling data and evidence
2. **Build a Coalition** — assemble a diverse, influential team to lead the effort
3. **Create a Vision** — develop a clear, inspiring picture of the desired future state
4. **Communicate the Vision** — use multiple channels to achieve shared understanding
5. **Remove Obstacles** — identify and clear structural, procedural, or cultural barriers
6. **Create Short-Term Wins** — celebrate early successes to build momentum
7. **Build on the Change** — integrate the change into culture, systems, and processes
8. **Anchor the Changes** — institutionalise new practices into permanent organisational culture

## Examples
- From [[Change Management - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]]: A logistics company adopting sustainable supply chain practices uses all 8 steps, ending by embedding eco-friendly values into performance evaluations.

## My thinking
Where [[Lewin Change Model]] gives a simple yet effective three-stage structure suitable for both planned and unplanned change, the [[Kotter 8-Step Model]] adds granularity within each stage for larger, more complex transformations. The [[ADKAR Model]] is internationally recognized through PROSCI certification and suitable for more complex planned institutional change management specialists dealing directly with [[Human Side of Change]].
