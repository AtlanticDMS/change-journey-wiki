## Definition
A management methodology focused on maximising customer value by systematically identifying and eliminating waste — non-value-added activities — throughout business processes, originally developed within manufacturing and later generalised across industries.

## What is this theme?

Internal improvements may be driven by a commitment to excellence and a pursuit of operational efficiency, and may also contribute to the inevitability of change within organizations. [[Continuous Improvement]] initiatives, fuelled by advancements in management philosophies like [[LEAN+]] or Six Sigma, foster a culture of innovation and efficiency.
## Why it matters
LEAN gives SMBs a disciplined lens for finding hidden inefficiency: excess inventory, unnecessary motion, waiting time, overproduction, defects, over-processing, and unused talent. For resource-constrained businesses, eliminating this waste is often a faster and cheaper route to competitiveness than large new investments.

## How it works
- **Value stream mapping** — visualising the end-to-end flow of a process to spot bottlenecks and non-value-added steps
- **Waste identification** — categorising inefficiency across the classic forms: overproduction, inventory, motion, defects, over-processing, waiting, and unused talent
- **Just-in-time principles** — reducing batch sizes and excess inventory to cut carrying costs and improve flow
- **Continuous refinement** — treating process improvement as ongoing rather than a one-off project

## Examples from the books
- From [[AI and Digital Transformation - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 12): a small manufacturing firm conducts value stream mapping, discovers overproduction is driving excess inventory, and implements just-in-time production to reduce waste.
- Referenced via [[Womack & Jones (1996)]]-style lean thinking in the Vol I literature review, outlining five lean principles: value, value stream, value flow, attraction (pull), and perfection.
