## Definition
The use of historical data, statistical algorithms, and machine learning models to forecast future outcomes — customer behaviour, market demand, churn probability, and revenue performance — with quantified probability rather than certainty.

## Why it matters
Predictive analytics shifts business decision-making from reactive (responding to what happened) to proactive (preparing for what is likely to happen). For SMBs, this is the difference between chasing trends and setting them. Once accessible only to large enterprises, it is now democratised through affordable AI tools.

## How it works
**Three-stage process:**
1. **Data collection and preparation** — gather from CRM (HubSpot), e-commerce (Shopify), social media (Google Analytics); clean thoroughly — "rubbish in, rubbish out"
2. **Model selection and training** — choose model type to match goal: decision trees for classification (churn/no churn), time-series for demand forecasting, regression for continuous outcomes; train on historical data
3. **Validation and deployment** — test with fresh data using cross-validation; deploy via dashboards (Tableau), API integrations into CRM, or real-time systems

**Seven marketing applications:**
- Customer segmentation (behavioural, not just demographic)
- Lead scoring (rank by conversion likelihood)
- Personalised marketing (right message, right moment)
- Demand forecasting (inventory and campaign planning)
- CLV prediction (identify high-value customers early)
- Churn prediction (spot at-risk customers before they leave)
- Pricing optimisation (dynamic offers based on demand signals)

**Key tools:** Google Cloud AI, Microsoft Azure ML, DataRobot, RapidMiner, Alteryx, Tableau, Power BI.
