## Definition
The systematic analysis of customer data to understand behaviour, predict future actions, personalise interactions, and optimise the customer lifecycle — from acquisition through retention and advocacy.

## Why it matters
Customer data is the richest and most actionable data most SMBs possess. Customer analytics converts this raw data into competitive advantage — enabling personalisation at scale, early identification of churn risk, and resource allocation toward highest-value customer segments.

## Key applications
- **Segmentation** — grouping customers by shared characteristics for targeted marketing (see [[Customer Segmentation]] Vol 2)
- **Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) prediction** — forecasting long-term value to guide retention investment
- **Churn prediction** — identifying at-risk customers before they leave; enabling proactive re-engagement
- **Personalised marketing** — anticipating individual needs to deliver relevant content at the right moment
- **Lead scoring** — ranking prospects by conversion likelihood to prioritise sales effort
- **Demand forecasting** — predicting future purchasing patterns from historical and contextual data
- **Pricing optimisation** — dynamically adjusting offers based on demand, competition, and customer sensitivity

## The Brew Haven case study
A fictional coffee shop implemented a data-driven loyalty app — tracking customer preferences, visit frequency, and purchase patterns. Result: 25% increase in customer retention and 15% revenue boost within six months. Key insight: personalised offers based on individual behaviour (not demographic assumptions) drove the results.
