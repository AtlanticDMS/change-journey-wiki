## Definition
The organisational infrastructure — tools, processes, roles, governance, and culture — that enables systematic collection, analysis, and actioning of data across an organisation. The operational backbone of a data-driven business.

## Why it matters
Without a framework, analytics is ad hoc — valuable insights get generated occasionally but are never systematically embedded into decisions. A framework converts analytics from a one-off exercise into an organisational capability.

## How it works
Key components:
1. **Define business questions** — start with the decisions that need to be made, not the data that is available
2. **Identify relevant data sources** — work backward from business questions to determine what data is needed
3. **Establish data collection processes** — integrate tools across touchpoints (CRM, web analytics, social, transaction systems)
4. **Build analysis capability** — select tools appropriate to team skill level; consider outsourcing until internal capability is built
5. **Create actionability loops** — establish processes for translating dashboard insights into operational decisions; avoid insight silos
6. **Cross-functional integration** — sales, marketing, operations, and finance should share data and analytics access; break down departmental silos
7. **Governance** — data naming conventions, access controls, quality standards, and update schedules
