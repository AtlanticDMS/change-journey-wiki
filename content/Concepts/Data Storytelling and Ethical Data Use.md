## Definition
The practice of translating analytical findings into compelling, accessible narratives that drive understanding and action — combining quantitative insights with qualitative context, visual representation, and human meaning.

## Why it matters
Data without narrative is inert. The majority of stakeholders — colleagues, customers, investors, leadership — respond to stories, not spreadsheets. Data storytelling is the bridge between analytical insight and organisational action.

## How it works
- Lead with the insight or implication, not the methodology
- Use visualisation to make patterns immediately apparent (charts, dashboards, heatmaps)
- Combine quantitative data with qualitative human context — customer quotes, case study anecdotes, real-world examples
- Structure the narrative: situation → complication → resolution (the classic story arc)
- Acknowledge uncertainty and data limitations honestly — credibility depends on it
- Tailor the level of technical detail to the audience

## Definition
The responsible collection, storage, analysis, and application of data in ways that respect individual privacy, comply with relevant regulations, and avoid discrimination or manipulation — treating data as a trust asset, not just an information asset.

## Why it matters
Data ethics is simultaneously a legal obligation, a competitive differentiator, and a trust foundation. Organisations that exploit customer data — even legally — risk reputational damage and customer defection. Those that handle data transparently and responsibly build lasting trust.

## How it works
**Key principles:**
- Transparency — be clear with customers about what data is collected and how it is used
- Consent — obtain informed consent; do not assume permission
- Minimisation — collect only what is needed for stated purposes; resist "collect everything" temptation
- Security — protect data from unauthorised access (see [[Cybersecurity]], Vol 2)
- Compliance — GDPR (EU/UK), CCPA (California), POPIA (South Africa), HIPAA (US healthcare), PCI DSS (payment data)
- Anti-discrimination — ensure analytics models do not encode or amplify discriminatory patterns

**Bias awareness in research:**
Biases can enter at any stage — collection (selection bias, social desirability bias), analysis (confirmation bias, hindsight bias), and interpretation (overconfidence, framing bias). See [[Cognitive Biases in Research]].
