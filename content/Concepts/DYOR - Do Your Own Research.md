## Definition
The practice of independently verifying information and conducting primary research before making major decisions, rather than relying on received wisdom, gut instinct, or unverified external sources. Originally popularised in cryptocurrency communities (2016–2018) as a warning against scams; now broadly applicable to business decision-making.

## Why it matters
In an era of information abundance, not all data is accurate, unbiased, or relevant. SMB leaders who outsource their critical thinking to trends, influencers, or consultants without independent verification consistently make worse decisions than those who build their own evidence base.

## How it works
DYOR in a business context means:
- Validating assumptions with primary data before committing resources
- Cross-referencing multiple data sources before drawing conclusions
- Applying healthy scepticism to any data that supports a pre-existing belief
- Building internal research and analytics capability rather than relying entirely on external reports
- Treating all incoming data — including third-party market research — as a starting point for verification, not a final answer
