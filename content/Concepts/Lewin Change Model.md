## Definition
A foundational three-stage model developed by Kurt Lewin for implementing organisational change. Simple enough to also be adapted for unplanned change situations.

## Why it matters
Its simplicity makes it universally applicable. It captures the key insight that change requires actively destabilising the current state before introducing something new — you cannot just layer new behaviour on top of old.

## How it works
1. **Unfreeze** — disrupt the existing equilibrium; create awareness of why change is needed; address resistance; identify key stakeholders
2. **Change** — implement new processes, structures, or technologies; communicate the vision; provide training; model desired leadership behaviours
3. **Refreeze** — stabilise the new state; celebrate wins; embed changes into culture; establish monitoring and feedback loops

## Examples
- From [[Change Management - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]]: A company digitising its operations unfreezes by communicating urgency around AI disruption, changes by rolling out digital tools and training, and refreezes by updating policies and performance metrics.

## My thinking
Where [[Lewin Change Model]] gives a simple yet effective three-stage structure suitable for both planned and unplanned change, the [[Kotter 8-Step Model]] adds granularity within each stage for larger, more complex transformations.
