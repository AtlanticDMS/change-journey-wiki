## Definition
The organisational mindset, values, behaviours, and practices that enable and sustain digital transformation. A digital culture is characterised by openness to change, data-driven decision-making, continuous learning, cross-functional collaboration, and a willingness to experiment and fail fast.

## Why it matters
Technology can be purchased; culture must be grown. Organisations that invest in technology without building the culture to use it effectively see minimal return. Digital culture is the human operating system that technology runs on.

## How it works
**Best practices for building digital culture:**
- Leaders must visibly model digital behaviours — not just endorse them (see [[Leadership Alignment]])
- Celebrate experimentation and learning from failure, not just success
- Create psychological safety so employees surface problems early
- Make digital literacy training ongoing, not a one-time event
- Establish cross-functional teams to break down departmental silos
- Communicate a clear "why" for transformation at every level (see [[Communication Strategy]])

**Educating and empowering employees:**
- Pair formal training with peer learning and mentorship
- Use champions — early adopters who model and advocate new tools
- Make skill-building relevant to employees' specific roles

**Addressing change management challenges within digital transformation:**
This chapter explicitly bridges back to Vol 1: resistance to technology adoption follows the same patterns as resistance to organisational change. The [[ADKAR Model]] (Vol 1) applies directly — employees must move through Awareness → Desire → Knowledge → Ability → Reinforcement for each new tool or process.
