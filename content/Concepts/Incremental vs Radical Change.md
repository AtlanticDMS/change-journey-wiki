## Definition
A foundational spectrum in change management theory distinguishing incremental change — small, gradual, cumulative adjustments — from radical change — transformative, abrupt shifts in strategy, structure, or culture.

## Why it matters
Understanding where a given change initiative sits on this spectrum determines the appropriate strategy, pace, and level of effort required. Radical change demands far greater stakeholder buy-in and disrupts ingrained norms, while incremental change, though less dramatic, is often neglected until problems compound and force a more painful, disruptive correction.

## How it works
- **Incremental change** — evolutionary, less disruptive; example: upgrading software systems in phases
- **Radical change** — transformative, often disruptive but necessary to stimulate innovation; example: a company pivoting from traditional manufacturing to AI-driven solutions
- **Not either/or** — most organisations benefit from a balanced approach, using incremental adjustments for operational efficiency and radical shifts to navigate genuinely disruptive forces
- **The neglect trap** — few organisations sustain effective continuous (incremental) improvement, and instead accumulate problems until a more disruptive, radical correction becomes unavoidable
