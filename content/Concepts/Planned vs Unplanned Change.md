## Definition
A second key axis in change classification, distinguishing planned change — deliberate, proactive initiatives driven by organisational strategy — from unplanned change — reactive responses to unforeseen external events or pressures.

## Why it matters
Each type demands a fundamentally different management posture. Planned change allows for careful preparation, stakeholder engagement, and structured methodology; unplanned change demands speed, agility, and resilience. SMBs with insufficient liquidity or flexibility to react to sudden change are identified in the series as being most at risk.

## How it works
- **Planned change** — intentional, systematic; example: implementing a new performance management system to improve productivity
- **Unplanned change** — reactive, urgent; example: adjusting operations swiftly in response to an economic downturn or competitor move
- **The reality gap** — the series notes that in practice, few organisations implement planned change effectively; continuous improvement is often neglected until damage forces more disruptive corrective action
- **Model fit** — the [[Lewin Change Model]] and [[ADKAR Model]] are highlighted as flexible enough to guide response even to unplanned change events, while [[Kotter 8-Step Model]] is generally suited to planned change

## Examples from the books
- From [[Change Management - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]] (Ch. 2): planned change is illustrated by deliberate performance management system rollouts; unplanned change by swift operational adjustment to economic downturns or competitive moves.
