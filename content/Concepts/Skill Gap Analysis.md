## Definition
A systematic 12-step process to identify the gap between current employee skills and the skills required after an organisational transformation — and to design targeted training to close that gap.

## Why it matters
Organisations consistently underestimate the cost and time required to train people into new roles. A proper skill gap analysis prevents this by making the gap visible before implementation begins. Re-skilling existing employees is far more cost-effective than hiring new talent.

## How it works
The 12-step framework:
1. Define organisational objectives for the transformation
2. Identify new roles and responsibilities emerging from the change
3. Assess current employee skills (surveys, interviews, performance reviews)
4. Compare current skills with required skills to identify gaps
5. Prioritise gaps by relevance and criticality
6. Identify specific training needs per gap
7. Design targeted training programs (modular, self-paced where appropriate)
8. Choose delivery methods (workshops, online, mentorship, on-the-job)
9. Implement training programs systematically and at a pace matched to absorption capacity
10. Monitor progress continuously through feedback and performance indicators
11. Provide ongoing support — coaching, mentorship, additional resources
12. Adjust programs based on feedback and evolving needs

Not all steps are always necessary; the transformation objectives determine which apply.
