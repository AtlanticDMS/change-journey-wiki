## Definition
A major branch of [[Artificial Intelligence]] in which systems learn and improve from experience — discovering patterns in data and making predictions or decisions — without being explicitly reprogrammed for each new scenario.

## Why it matters
ML is the engine behind the most commercially impactful AI applications: recommendation systems, demand forecasting, fraud detection, and predictive analytics. Most SMB AI tools are ML-powered under the hood.

## How it works
ML systems are trained on historical data. The algorithm identifies patterns and builds a model. When given new data, the model applies learned patterns to make predictions or classifications. The more data, the more accurate the model — creating a compounding advantage for data-rich organisations.

## Business examples from the book
- Netflix: ML analyses viewing patterns to generate personalised recommendations
- RepVue: ML rates companies' sales compensation based on real-time data patterns
- Demand forecasting: ML correlates historical sales with market variables to predict future inventory needs
