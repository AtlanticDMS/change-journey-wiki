## Definition
A structured roadmap that defines an organisation's digital transformation goals, prioritises initiatives based on impact and feasibility, and establishes a sequenced implementation plan aligned to strategic business objectives.

## Why it matters
Without a strategy, digital adoption becomes a series of disconnected tool purchases with no cumulative value. Strategy transforms spending into investment and activity into progress.

## How it works
**Defining strategic goals** — goals must be SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) and tied directly to business outcomes. Common goal categories:
- Operational efficiency (cost reduction, process speed)
- Customer experience enhancement
- Revenue growth through new digital channels
- Data capability building
- Risk and compliance management

**Prioritising initiatives** — use an impact vs feasibility matrix:
- High impact, high feasibility → implement first (quick wins)
- High impact, low feasibility → plan carefully, invest in enablers
- Low impact, high feasibility → consider if resources allow
- Low impact, low feasibility → deprioritise or discard

**Sequencing** — begin with non-critical workloads and internal tools before migrating customer-facing or mission-critical systems. The ByteBright parable in the book illustrates this: start with email and video conferencing, build confidence, then tackle CRM and core systems.
