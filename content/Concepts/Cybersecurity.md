## Definition
The practice of protecting systems, networks, data, and programmes from digital attacks, unauthorised access, damage, or theft. In the context of digital transformation, cybersecurity is a non-negotiable parallel investment to every technology adoption decision.

## Why it matters
Every step of digital transformation expands the attack surface. Cloud migration, remote working, customer data collection, and third-party integrations all introduce new vulnerabilities. Regulatory penalties, reputational damage, and operational disruption from a breach can exceed the cost of the transformation itself.

## How it works
**Key best practices from the book:**
- Implement a Zero Trust security model — verify every user and device, inside or outside the network
- Use multi-factor authentication (MFA) across all systems
- Encrypt data at rest and in transit (SSL/TLS protocols)
- Deploy Identity and Access Management (IAM) — role-based access control (RBAC)
- Conduct regular vulnerability assessments and penetration testing
- Maintain updated intrusion detection systems (IDS)
- Establish incident response plans before incidents occur

**Data privacy and compliance:**
- GDPR (EU and UK) — strict data protection and consent requirements
- HIPAA (US healthcare) — patient data protection
- PCI DSS — payment card data security
- POPIA (South Africa) — Protection of Personal Information Act
- Regulations vary by region; multi-jurisdictional businesses must comply with all applicable frameworks

**Employee awareness:**
Human error is the most common cybersecurity vulnerability. Regular training, phishing simulations, and clear protocols reduce this risk significantly. Cybersecurity is a culture issue as much as a technology issue.
