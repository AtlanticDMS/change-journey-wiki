## Definition
A proprietary, individual-focused change model developed by Jeff Hiatt. ADKAR is an acronym for the five sequential elements required for an individual to successfully adopt change: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement.

## Why it matters
Unlike Kotter or Lewin, which focus on the organisational level, the [[ADKAR Model]] addresses what happens inside each person during a change. This makes it especially useful for diagnosing where resistance is occurring — and why.

## Compliance
To use the ADKAR Model commercially, you may be required to complete PROSCI certification.
## How it works
1. **Awareness** — the individual understands *why* the change is needed; leaders communicate urgency and rationale
2. **Desire** — the individual is motivated to support the change; leaders address concerns and communicate benefits
3. **Knowledge** — the individual has the information and skills to implement the change; achieved through training and documentation
4. **Ability** — the individual can consistently apply new skills in daily work; built through practice, trial periods, and mentorship
5. **Reinforcement** — the new behaviour is sustained through recognition, feedback, and celebration of successes

## Examples
- From [[Change Management - A Guidebook for SMB Leaders]]: A company rolling out a new digital project management system walks each employee through all five ADKAR stages — from communicating the need, to training, to sandbox trials, to recognising early adopters.

## My thinking
Where [[Lewin Change Model]] gives a simple yet effective three-stage structure suitable for both planned and unplanned change, the [[Kotter 8-Step Model]] adds granularity within each stage for larger, more complex transformations. The [[ADKAR Model]] is internationally recognized through PROSCI certification and suitable for more complex planned institutional change management specialists dealing directly with [[Cultural Sensitivity in Organisations]].
