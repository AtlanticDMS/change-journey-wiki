## Definition
The ability to understand and navigate cultural differences effectively, enabling individuals and organisations to collaborate across diverse cultural contexts. Often referred to as CQ (Cultural Quotient).

## Why it matters
Change management applied in multicultural contexts will fail without cultural intelligence. Communication styles, decision-making preferences, attitudes toward authority, and responses to change all vary significantly across cultures. A one-size-fits-all approach consistently produces resistance and disengagement.

## How it works
Key dimensions to understand and apply:

- **Communication styles** — direct vs indirect; explicit vs implicit messaging
- **Hierarchical structures** — who has decision-making authority and how decisions flow
- **Collectivism vs individualism** — whether group harmony or individual achievement drives motivation
- **Attitudes toward change** — some cultures embrace innovation; others value stability and tradition

Collectivist cultures (e.g. China, Japan, many African and Latin American countries) prioritise group harmony and indirect communication. Individualist cultures (e.g. USA, Australia, Western Europe) value personal achievement and direct communication. These are generalisations — real cultural diversity exists within every culture.
