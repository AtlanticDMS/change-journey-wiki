## Definition
The systematic collection, analysis, and interpretation of data about markets, customers, competitors, and marketing performance to support strategic and tactical marketing decisions. The evolved form of traditional market research.

## Why it matters
Marketing intelligence transforms marketing from an art into a discipline. Businesses that operate with strong marketing intelligence capabilities consistently outperform those that rely on creative instinct alone — not because creativity doesn't matter, but because intelligence ensures creative resources are deployed where they will have maximum impact.

## How it works
**Historical evolution:**
1. **Traditional era** (pre-digital) — focus groups, manual surveys, sales ledger data. Insightful but slow, expensive, and not scalable
2. **Digital era** — online surveys, web analytics, CRM data. Faster, more scalable, but still primarily retrospective
3. **Real-time analytics era** — predictive capabilities, AI-driven insights, democratised tools (Google Analytics, HubSpot, Power BI). Proactive and forward-looking
4. **Current era** — AI and ML as standard tooling; personalisation at individual scale; affordable for SMBs

**Key intelligence categories:**
- Customer intelligence — who they are, what they want, how they behave
- Market intelligence — trends, size, growth, emerging segments
- Competitive intelligence — competitor positioning, pricing, strategy
- Campaign intelligence — what marketing activity is working and why
