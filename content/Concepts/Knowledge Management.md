## Definition
In a commercial context, **knowledge management (KM)** is the systematic process of creating, capturing, organizing, storing, sharing, and applying an organization’s knowledge and information to improve performance, drive innovation, and sustain competitive advantage.

## Core definition in business

Commercial knowledge management treats knowledge as a strategic asset. It focuses on:

- **Identifying** valuable tacit (in people’s heads) and explicit (documents, data) knowledge  
- **Organizing and storing** it in accessible systems (intranets, wikis, knowledge bases, CRM, etc.)  
- **Sharing** it across teams, locations, and roles  
- **Applying** it to decision-making, problem-solving, product development, customer service, and process improvement

The goal is not just “having information,” but ensuring the right knowledge reaches the right people at the right time to create value. 

## Link to change management

Change management deals with how organizations prepare for, implement, and sustain changes in strategy, structure, processes, or technology. KM is tightly linked because:

- **Change creates new knowledge** (lessons learned, new ways of working) that must be captured and disseminated.  
- **Effective change depends on knowledge flow**: people can only adopt new behaviors if they understand *why* things are changing and *how* to do things differently.  
- KM supports **organizational learning**, which is essential for embedding change and avoiding “re-learning” the same lessons in future initiatives.

In practice, KM activities (post-project reviews, communities of practice, updated playbooks, training materials) are key mechanisms that make change stick.

## Link to digital transformation

Digital transformation is the deep, technology-enabled redesign of business models, processes, and customer experiences. KM is increasingly seen as a **foundational pillar** of digital transformation, alongside infrastructure and API management. [smjournal](https://www.smjournal.rs/index.php/home/article/download/135/142)

The relationship works in both directions:

- **Digital transformation enables KM**:  
  - Cloud platforms, collaboration tools, AI search, and data analytics make it easier to capture, index, retrieve, and share knowledge at scale.
  - Digital channels turn scattered documents and emails into structured, searchable knowledge assets.

- **KM enables digital transformation**:  
  - Without clear visibility into existing knowledge (process know-how, customer insights, domain expertise), organizations struggle to decide *what* to transform and *how*.
  - KM provides the “knowledge base” that digital tools automate, augment, and operationalize (e.g., chatbots powered by curated knowledge articles, AI-assisted decision support).

Strategically, KM and digital transformation share the same aim: continuously enhancing the organization’s capacity to create and deliver value by leveraging its knowledge assets.

## Putting it together

- **Knowledge management** = disciplined handling of organizational knowledge as a strategic resource.  
- **Change management** = ensuring people and processes adapt; KM supplies the knowledge needed to understand and sustain change.  
- **Digital transformation** = using technology to reinvent the business; KM both powers and is powered by this transformation, acting as a core enabler and beneficiary.

In modern commercial settings, effective KM is not a standalone “documentation project”; it’s a critical capability that underpins successful change initiatives and digital transformation programs.
