## Definition
Two complementary methodologies for managing work and improving processes. Agile focuses on iterative delivery, cross-functional collaboration, and responsiveness to change. Lean focuses on eliminating waste and maximising value through continuous incremental improvement (Kaizen).

## Why it matters
Post-transformation, organisations need a way to keep improving without launching another large-scale change initiative. Agile and Lean provide the operating rhythm for continuous improvement — making adaptation a daily habit rather than a periodic crisis.

## How it works
**Agile principles in transformation:**
- Break large initiatives into small, deliverable sprints (2–4 weeks)
- Review and adapt after every sprint — don't wait for the full project to complete
- Cross-functional teams own delivery end-to-end
- Customer feedback is incorporated continuously, not just at the end
- Faster time-to-market; earlier value delivery

**Lean principles:**
- Identify and eliminate the 8 wastes: overproduction, waiting, unnecessary transport, over-processing, excess inventory, unnecessary motion, defects, unused talent
- Kaizen — small, daily improvements compound into significant gains over time
- Lean Six Sigma combines waste reduction (Lean) with quality improvement (Six Sigma)
- Value stream mapping — visually map every step in a process to identify non-value-adding steps
