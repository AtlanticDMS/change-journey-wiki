## Definition
An organisational culture in which data and evidence are the primary inputs into decision-making at every level — from the C-suite to frontline employees. Characterised by decisions guided by evidence, empowered employees with data access, and openness to experimentation and learning from failure.

## Why it matters
Tools and frameworks alone do not create data-driven organisations — culture does. An organisation can have world-class analytics infrastructure and still make gut-based decisions if the culture doesn't value evidence. Cultural adoption is the last mile of analytics transformation.

## How it works
**Four characteristics of a data-driven culture:**
1. Decisions guided by evidence, not seniority or intuition
2. Employees empowered with access to relevant data for their role
3. Openness to experimentation — failure treated as learning, not liability
4. Data incorporated into performance evaluation at all levels

**Five strategies for building the culture:**
1. **Leadership commitment** — leaders must visibly use data in their own decision-making; champion analytics publicly
2. **Communication** — share success stories and explain the "why" behind data initiatives; connect data to outcomes employees care about
3. **Accessibility** — user-friendly dashboards (Tableau, Google Data Studio); no-code tools; knowledge management platforms with GenAI search
4. **Training and support** — workshops, peer mentoring, role-specific analytics training
5. **Reward systems** — recognise and reward data-driven decisions visibly; incorporate data-centric thinking into performance reviews

**Overcoming resistance:** Start with one department; demonstrate quick wins; use pilot projects; involve all stakeholders early; use cross-functional teams to create internal champions.
