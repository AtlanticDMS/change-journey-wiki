## Definition
A hybrid management methodology that combines LEAN's focus on eliminating waste with Six Sigma's statistical approach to reducing process variation and defects, aimed at achieving both operational efficiency and consistent quality.

## What is this theme?

Internal improvements may be driven by a commitment to excellence and a pursuit of operational efficiency, and may also contribute to the inevitability of change within organizations. [[Continuous Improvement]] initiatives, fuelled by advancements in management philosophies like [[LEAN+]] or Six Sigma, foster a culture of innovation and efficiency.
## Why it matters
Where pure LEAN targets speed and flow, Lean Six Sigma adds a rigorous quality dimension — useful for SMBs in regulated or precision-sensitive industries where inconsistency, not just slowness, is the core business risk. It is cited in the series as one of the continuous improvement philosophies that fuels internal drivers of organisational change.

## How it works
- Combines LEAN's waste-elimination tools with Six Sigma's DMAIC cycle (Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control)
- Targets both process speed (LEAN) and process consistency/defect reduction (Six Sigma) simultaneously
- Frequently used as an internal driver of continuous improvement initiatives, separate from externally-triggered change
