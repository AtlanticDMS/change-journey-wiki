## Definition
A flexible, iterative approach to project management and software development that emphasises incremental delivery, collaboration, and adaptability to change, breaking large initiatives into short, manageable cycles known as sprints.

## Why it matters
Agile gives SMBs a way to reduce the risk of large-scale failure inherent in long, waterfall-style development cycles. By delivering functional increments and gathering feedback continuously, businesses can course-correct early rather than discovering a mismatch with customer needs only at the end of a lengthy build.

## How it works
- **Iterative development** — projects are broken into sprints (commonly two-week cycles), each producing a working increment
- **Continuous feedback** — user input is collected at the end of each sprint and used to adjust the next cycle's priorities
- **Retrospectives** — regular reflection sessions where teams discuss what went well, what didn't, and what to change
- **Cross-functional collaboration** — agile teams typically pull members from multiple departments to widen the pool of perspectives
