## Definition
A German-American psychologist (1890–1947), widely regarded as one of the founders of social psychology and organisational development. Best known in change management circles for his three-stage model of change and his broader work on field theory and group dynamics.

## Why it matters
Lewin's Unfreeze-Change-Refreeze model remains one of the most widely taught and applied change frameworks nearly 75 years after his death, precisely because of its simplicity and adaptability. His foundational insight — that behaviour is a function of the person and their environment (field theory) — underpins much of modern organisational change theory, including later, more granular models like Kotter's.

## How it works
Lewin's contribution to change management rests on a small number of durable ideas:
- **Field theory** — behaviour results from the interaction between a person and their social environment, not from individual disposition alone
- **Force field analysis** — change occurs when driving forces for change outweigh restraining forces maintaining the status quo
- **The three-stage change model** — Unfreeze, Change, Refreeze — describing the process of destabilising, transitioning, and restabilising a social system
