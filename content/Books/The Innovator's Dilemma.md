## Definition
A 1997 business book by Clayton M. Christensen, listed in the Recommended Reading section of the Change Journey series. Introduces the theory of disruptive innovation — explaining why successful, well-managed incumbent companies can nonetheless be overtaken by simpler, cheaper technologies that initially appear inferior.

## Why it matters
Christensen's theory provides the intellectual backbone for the series' repeated warnings, especially in Vol I and Vol II, about companies that fail to adapt to disruptive change (Blockbuster, Kodak, Nokia, BlackBerry are all cited as cautionary tales in Vol I Ch. 6). It explains *why* rational, competent management can still lead to obsolescence if digital or market disruption is ignored.

## How it works
- Distinguishes sustaining innovations (improve existing products for existing customers) from disruptive innovations (initially worse, but cheaper/simpler, eventually overtaking the incumbent)
- Explains why incumbents' own rational resource-allocation processes make them slow to respond to disruption
- Frames disruption as a structural, predictable pattern rather than a matter of bad management
